# Sprint 13: prospective diary feasibility pilot — draft protocol v0.1

Prepared 2026-10-27. **Planning document, not approved for recruitment or real health-data collection.** Responsible PI, institution, jurisdiction, sponsor, ethics reference, data controller, study site and operational contacts are all **TBD**. No participant has been enrolled. No model will be trained, deployed or displayed by this pilot. See [readiness gate](RESEARCH_READINESS_GATE.md) and [external acquisition plan](RESEARCH_DATA_ACCESS.md).

## Purpose and design

Assess whether a low-burden diary process can retain adults, produce explicit completed-interval reports, preserve genuine availability/revisions and support independently reviewable outcome evidence. The endpoints are collection feasibility and evidence quality, not predictive accuracy, clinical benefit, causal triggers or treatment response. Descriptive associations would require a separately approved analysis specification.

Proposed single-site, noninterventional design: **12–20 adults**, **7-day onboarding/run-in plus 56 days of observation**, followed by a short exit interview. This convenience size is an operational planning choice, not powered for forecasting or a clinical effect. Retain run-in/noncompletion/withdrawal denominators; never require future adherence to include earlier records in the feasibility cohort. Total person-days cannot substitute for per-person coverage. Even perfect follow-up here cannot pass the existing baseline's 180-day/180-window engineering gates; changing those gates is outside this sprint. A larger/longer forecasting study would need a separate protocol and design justification.

Proposed recruitment, only after approvals: an institution-approved invitation through a collaborating clinical service, followed by voluntary contact with a research coordinator separate from treatment decisions. No scraping app users, automatic enrollment, mailing existing users, coercive clinician invitation or use of current private app records. Invitations describe uncertain research value and no monitoring/medical benefit. Compensation, if any, must be institution-approved, proportionate and not dependent on migraine events or complete diaries; earned compensation is not lost on withdrawal. Record consented/enrolled/started counts without collecting identifiers for people who decline unless approved.

## Eligibility and participant burden

Include adults aged 18 or older, able to provide informed consent, with an existing clinician-established migraine history documented by the approved study team, access to a compatible personal device and an approved-language information sheet/diary they can understand with reasonable accessibility support. This pilot does not diagnose participants. Screening verifies eligibility through minimal coded confirmation; do not copy entire medical charts. Ability to complete every day or a minimum event count is not required.

Exclude inability to consent, absence of the approved clinical eligibility confirmation, lack of a safe supported access route, or concurrent study participation where the PI identifies a consent/data conflict. Minors and other groups needing protections beyond this initial protocol are not enrolled under v0.1. Do not exclude people merely for frequent attacks, unknown entries or low completion; describe recruitment and device/language limitations. Define phenotype subgroup handling before recruitment rather than excluding chronic migraine using future diary outcomes. No medication changes or provocative exposures are requested. Any new clinical concern follows usual care outside the research system.

| Time | Proposed activity | Burden and fallback |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Before day 1 | Consent, eligibility, privacy explanation, timezone/accessibility setup and practice with synthetic examples | 20–30 minutes; no collection before consent activation |
| Run-in days 1–7 | Practice real study entries only after all approvals; measure usability and timing problems | Same schedule as follow-up; retain failures in feasibility reporting |
| Each morning, preferred 08:00–12:00 local | Explicit review of the preceding completed local day; migraine occurred / no migraine / unknown; separate ongoing-state question; interval gaps/conflicts | Target 1–2 minutes, optional measures skippable; never default no migraine |
| When tolerable after onset/recovery | Optional short event report with event time, uncertainty and ongoing/resolved state; later/backdated reporting permitted | Target under 1 minute per event, no requirement during pain; delayed reports retain actual server time |
| Optional daily observations | Sleep duration/quality, stress and hydration using existing units; no required free text, location or wearables | Optional, not needed for completion; cannot claim proven triggers |
| Once weekly | Coordinator checks missing/ambiguous entries and burden, without requesting guessed answers | Target up to 5 minutes, participant may decline; late clarification does not backdate availability |
| End or withdrawal | Optional usability/retention interview | Target 10 minutes; right to omit |

Maximum one participant-configured generic reminder per day during approved waking hours, with opt-out and no health details on lock screens. This reminder and study schedule are **proposed operations**, not features implemented here. No repeated nagging, required streaks or completeness rewards. Existing Attack Mode/appearance, mobile access and keyboard workflows should be tested with synthetic accounts before approval; migraine-time comfort and assistive-technology suitability remain unverified. A day can be reported unknown without explanation. No extra observation should be requested just to improve a quality metric.

## Operational outcome definitions

These are proposed research coding rules, not diagnostic criteria. A qualified clinical investigator must approve phenotype, aura/probable-migraine handling, recurrence rules and evidence standard before activation. Recording a self-reported migraine and verifying an exact new onset are different actions.

| Concept | Proposed rule and evidence boundary |
| --- | --- |
| Structurally valid observation | Consented owner; stable record/date/source zone; genuine server creation and ordered revision chain; valid reported event/observation times and explicit missing values. Structural validation is not clinical or independent verification. |
| Completed-day report | Participant explicitly reviews a named source-local day after its next midnight, with confirmed coverage or marked gaps/uncertainty. An earlier statement is provisional. This is a participant statement, not independently verified full-horizon coverage. |
| Candidate migraine onset | Participant-reported beginning of a new episode, distinct from diary submission, with exact/uncertain event time and an episode identifier; investigator applies approved classification without exposing predictors or model outputs. Aura/headache start must remain separately typed in the study journal until the target rule is approved. |
| New versus ongoing | Operational candidate-new rule: at least 24 elapsed hours of explicitly observed migraine-free recovery before reported onset; otherwise continuation/uncertain. This is a conservative proposed research convention, not a validated clinical recurrence rule. Unobserved recovery makes onset unresolved. Record recovery separately; never manufacture a midnight start/end or final duration. |
| Independently verified onset | A trained reviewer, independent of feature preparation, documents separate protocol-accepted contemporaneous corroborating evidence, its availability, exact-onset uncertainty, classification version and adjudication time. Re-reading the same diary, copying a daily status, or setting a Boolean is not independent verification. If no defensible separate evidence exists, retain self-report only and leave the strict outcome unverified. |
| Observation interval | Explicit start/end with documented surveillance method, complete/gapped state, uncertainty and independent evidence references. An onset report alone does not establish coverage of the rest of its horizon. |
| Explicit no-attack interval | Affirmative post-interval report of no migraine throughout a named interval, no contradictory ongoing/onset evidence, with independently reviewable completeness evidence under the approved protocol. A wearable stream alone does not verify absence. The existing app's completed no-migraine day is insufficient by itself. |
| Unknown outcome | Any gap, unsure answer, missing day, unresolved ongoing attack, uncertain boundary crossing, invalid timestamp, inconsistent evidence, deletion or unverifiable history. Exclude strict labels; retain coded quality reasons where consent permits. No imputation as negative. |

**Outcome verification is a central feasibility question, not a promised capability.** A candidate separate source could be a time-stamped participant event log retained independently from the feature diary plus a structured next-day reconciliation record. Both remain human self-report and may have correlated recall error. The ethics/clinical team must judge whether they provide meaningful independent corroboration; separate storage or a coordinator signature alone does not qualify them. Do not require intrusive surveillance or clinical visits to prove continuous absence. If this standard cannot be met ethically at acceptable burden, the strict forecasting task stays NO-GO even with excellent diary completion.

The independent journal would need pseudonymous owner/episode IDs, source event times and uncertainty, coverage boundaries/gaps, actual source receipt and revision times, evidence references, reviewer/adjudication times, decision/reason and protocol version. Sensitive evidence remains in approved restricted storage. A second reviewer resolves disagreement without overwriting either decision; unresolved conflict is unknown. No adverse inference is drawn from an inability to corroborate a report. Every interval used for a strict label needs review, not just an audited sample. Double-review a prespecified 20% of reviewed intervals, including positive/negative/unknown cases, for agreement; this operational fraction is not proof of truth.

## Time, historical availability and labels

Proposed analysis-only schedule: choose one UTC anchor per participant at enrollment corresponding to approximately 09:00 in their enrollment zone, and keep **24 elapsed hours** between cutoffs. DST/travel may shift displayed local time; display that clearly. Reminders and completed-day reports follow source-local days, which may be 23/25 hours. Cutoffs are not frontend risk updates and do not imply an inference service. Freeze the planned candidate grid before outcomes and keep all reasons for excluded cutoffs. Plan follow-up cutoffs only where the entire horizon ends within the scheduled consented observation period; record run-in cutoffs separately. Allow up to 48 hours after planned observation closure for optional final completed-day confirmation and staff adjudication, disclosed in consent; this does not extend event surveillance. Do not request additional reports after withdrawal. An uncompleted horizon or evidence unavailable by freeze remains excluded, with a reason, rather than being removed silently from the planned denominator.

- Horizon: `[t, t + 86,400 seconds)`; at-risk only when no prior ongoing/unresolved attack is known, with retrospectively revealed ongoing state handled by the outcome-freeze policy. Onset exactly at `t` is in; onset exactly at the horizon end is out. Boundary-spanning onset uncertainty excludes the label. Local-day coverage must be converted from documented zone boundaries, never assumed 24 hours or assigned a zone from location.
- Features: reuse the [Sprint 10 allowlist and contract](ML_RESEARCH_BASELINE.md), not legacy diary/wellness final rows. Select the latest eligible revision with genuine server `recorded_at < t`; equal-to-cutoff reports are unavailable. Only previous completed source-local-day measurements and approved history qualify. Optional missing values remain null. Exclude free text, target-period symptoms, final duration, treatment response, exit surveys and future person averages. The pilot does not fit a model or expand the allowlist.
- Proposed label/eligibility freeze: horizon end plus **24 hours**, matching the current baseline default; this is an operational assumption to test, not proof reports are complete. Positive requires independently verified onset within the horizon **and** full independently verified observation coverage. Negative requires continuous, complete, explicit independently verified no-episode coverage throughout. A one-second gap or unresolved conflict excludes the window. A headache subtype flag or missing report is not negative coverage.
- Use actual availability of source evidence and adjudication, not only source event times. If daily review staffing cannot finalize evidence by freeze, record late/unknown and exclude; do not move a late decision back to meet the deadline. Weekly coordinator checks assess quality and cannot supply earlier availability. Estimate daily adjudication workload in a synthetic rehearsal and fund named coverage before activation; no unattended adjudication is implied.
- Maintain separate historical features, frozen outcome adjudication and frozen build eligibility. A correction/deletion known before the applicable boundary affects that view; later corrections cannot rewrite it or silently remove an earlier window. Never delete original audit versions to make history agree. A later safety/withdrawal restriction can require revoking an entire research snapshot: document the legal/access reason and issue a new version, not a silent historical reclassification.
- Record protocol and source versions, consent scope/version, genuine revision availability, `captured_at`, dataset-as-of, per-window feature snapshot, `outcome_as_of`, `eligibility_as_of`, label availability, manifest hashes and planned exclusions. Incomplete/truncated history excludes reconstruction. Hashes do not recover erased evidence or prove its truth.

No real research bundle is constructed in this sprint. The current adapter supplies zero verified outcomes; no export confirmation or reviewer role magically changes that. A future reviewed journal/consent infrastructure is essential and must undergo implementation and synthetic regression testing before recruitment.

## Feasibility metrics and decision rules

Prespecify these operational targets with the PI/statistician and participant advisers before enrollment. They are **proposals**, not measured results, clinical readiness standards or proof of sufficient events. Report counts, denominators, per-participant distributions and uncertainty; small samples do not support precise effectiveness claims. Optional missing fields are never counted as failed mandatory diary completion.

| Metric | Definition / denominator | Proposed proceed-to-design target |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Retention | Participants reaching day 56 follow-up / all enrolled; reasons and time-to-withdrawal separately | At least 80%; do not remove withdrawals from denominator |
| Completed-day statements | Eligible completed-day confirmations / all scheduled days while consent active, including missed days; run-in separate | Cohort at least 80%, and at least 75% of participants individually reach 70% |
| Timeliness | Confirmations received within 24 hours after source-day end / all scheduled days; receipt-delay distribution among submitted reports | At least 75%; report both denominators and late proportions |
| Missing/unknown coverage | Days and elapsed time missing, explicit unknown, provisional, contradictory, deleted and only partially covered / scheduled days or monitored elapsed time, respectively | No zero-filling; investigate distributions and reasons, not only pooled averages |
| Structural timestamp/revision integrity | Reports with valid chronology, timezone and complete creation-anchored chains / submitted reports; actual correction/deletion counts | At least 99% structurally valid; zero unresolved systemic availability failures |
| Independently adjudicable horizons | Full-horizon coverage and evidence finalized by freeze / all planned cutoffs during active consent, with separate positive/negative/unknown counts and exclusions | At least 70% overall and at least half of participants reach 60%; no forced event/class quota |
| Review reliability | Agreement and unresolved conflicts among prespecified double-reviewed intervals; report sample/class counts and timing | All conflicts resolved or excluded; no arbitrary agreement percentage equated with validity |
| Burden and staffing | Self-reported minutes, optional omissions, reminder opt-outs, coordinator/reviewer minutes and migraine-time usability complaints | Median daily burden at most 3 minutes; majority find it acceptable; sustainable reviewed staffing plan |
| Privacy and isolation | Incident count/severity, access reviews and cross-account test results | Zero unresolved serious incidents; any cross-account exposure pauses collection |

Report scheduled days before withdrawal separately from full intended follow-up to expose attrition bias. Days after withdrawal are not missing surveillance to be collected; do not contact for completion. Track onset time uncertainty, unverified onsets, gap duration, travel/DST issues and exclusions at each freeze. Feasibility denominators cannot be restricted to successful exports or retained labels. Report zero/single-class cases plainly, with no PR-AUC, Brier or fabricated performance metrics because no prediction experiment occurs.

If approval/security/integrity fails: **NO-GO**. If burden, retention or coverage misses targets: **REVISE** diary/process and seek an amendment before another pilot. If independent outcome verification fails: **NO-GO for strict onset forecasting**, even if descriptive collection is feasible. If all pilot targets pass: **CONDITIONAL GO only to design a later study**, with a defensible event/sample-size and duration assessment, temporal dependence, planned purged splits, participant isolation, training-only preprocessing, naive comparators and prespecified uncertainty. No automatic training, deployment or clinical-validation decision follows. Event counts and class balance may still make every individual unevaluable.

## Consent, privacy and operations — prerequisites, not implemented features

Use a separate versioned research-enrollment process, reviewed information sheet and consent comprehension check, with named controller and contact, aims, risks, absence of benefit, data categories, exact-date linkage risk, optional fields, retention, recipients, permitted analyses and withdrawal limits. Separate enrollment, optional recontact, future reuse and any export/transfer choices; no bundled caregiver consent or default opt-in. Existing one-download confirmation is not enrollment. Keep identity/contact/consent linkage separate from coded diary data. No precise location, third-party contacts, free text, full date of birth or raw clinical charts are required; collect only necessary eligibility confirmation and age band.

Withdrawal stops reminders, collection and new research use promptly; no reason required. Confirm whether the person also requests deletion of app records. Explain before consent that downloaded/approved archived copies, required institutional records and already published nonidentifying aggregates may have different lawful deletion limits. Do not promise that account deletion removes all copies. Map every copy and derivative, revoke access, record authorized destruction and prevent deleted data returning from restore. Whether previous study records may be retained must be explicitly resolved by the institution, consent and applicable law, not assumed here.

Proposed retention schedule for review: delete temporary working extracts within 30 days of verification; remove unnecessary direct recontact details within 90 days of study closure unless separately consented; retain minimum coded study/audit records up to 24 months after final report; expire encrypted rolling backups within 90 days. These are proposed limits, **not implemented timers or established legal periods**. The privacy/records offices must approve or replace them with a justified, participant-disclosed schedule before enrollment, including consent records and withdrawal tombstones. If a required retention rule conflicts, resolve before consent rather than promise incompatible erasure. No retention owner/schedule approval exists yet.

Require institutional managed hosting with HTTPS, encryption at rest and in transit, key management, protected backups and tested restores, verified identity/access recovery, least privilege/MFA for staff, shared persistent rate limits and security review. Participant reads/edits stay account-isolated. Coordinator access is scoped by approved assignments; reviewer sees minimum outcome evidence, analyst sees coded allowlisted histories, and identity linkage is custodian-only. Staff roles and immutable/tamper-evident audit storage are not supplied by today's prototype. No production shared-database admin shortcut or cross-account research CLI authorization is implied. Audit access without health values in logs; disable health telemetry and external AI-service upload. Secure managed transfer, access expiry and aggregate disclosure review are required for any future external collaboration.

Name PI, clinical reviewer, coordinator, data steward, security and privacy leads before starting. For suspected privacy breach or corrupted availability: suspend affected collection/export, contain access, preserve restricted incident evidence without logging health payloads, notify institutional leads immediately, assess impact and follow applicable reporting/participant-notification requirements. No legal deadline is invented here; the institution must establish it before launch. Resume only after documented authorization and corrective verification. Clinical concerns follow usual care and an approved escalation contact; the diary is not monitored for emergencies or intended to replace care, and no prediction controls emergency access.
